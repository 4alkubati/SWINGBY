# 15 — Tips and Workarounds

> The complete operating list: what to do, what to skip, and the shortcuts that actually work for a two-sided local marketplace. Built from the founder's own growth notes, translated to SwingBy.

## The one adjustment that changes everything below

The growth notes this page is built from are **creator playbooks** — written for someone building an audience they later monetise. SwingBy is not that. It's a two-sided local marketplace in one city, and the two sides need opposite tactics:

| | Supply side (businesses) | Demand side (clients) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | ~250 Calgary cleaners, handymen, dog walkers, movers | Calgary homeowners and renters, 22–45 |
| Won by | **Direct outreach. Phone, DM, in person.** | Content, local SEO, referral, paid |
| Social's role | Credibility check before they reply — nothing more | The actual acquisition channel |
| Realistic first 50 | 50 conversations | Comes free once supply exists |

**The first 250 businesses will not come from Instagram.** They come from a list and a phone. Social media's job on the supply side is to make a business owner who Googles "is SwingBy legit" find something that looks real. That's a 10-post bar, not a 1,000-follower bar.

Everything in the notes about follower growth applies to the **demand side only** — and even there, in Calgary, Google Business Profile and neighbourhood Facebook groups outperform Instagram reach for local services.

The notes' own best line is the one to keep: *would you rather have a million followers who never buy, or 1,000 who buy everything?* For SwingBy the honest target is smaller still — **250 businesses and a few hundred active clients in one city.** Every vanity metric below that number is a distraction.

## Part 1 — The founder's notes, translated

Each principle from the notes, with what it concretely means here.

### Ask first — the first ten come from people you know

Unchanged, and it's the highest-ROI hour available. Personal network, Calgary edition:
- Every tradesperson, cleaner, or contractor Amr has ever hired → "I built this, will you list on it free?"
- Every friend who owns or rents a home → "post one job, it's free, tell me where it breaks"
- Ask for the **specific action**, not support: *"Post a job by Sunday"* beats *"check it out"*.

This is also the highest-quality beta testing available, and it's free.

### Profile description = hook + offer

Locked in [13-accounts-and-identity.md](13-accounts-and-identity.md). Same bio everywhere, link to `swingbyy.com`, no Linktree.

### Building on rented land — get them onto email

Correct and urgent. Algorithm changes and the audience is gone; an email list is owned forever. Concretely:
- Waitlist capture on the launch site (`waitlist-launch-email.md` already drafted)
- Resend is already keyed — it just needs domain verification ([13](13-accounts-and-identity.md))
- **Every business that signs up is an email address we own.** That list is more valuable than any follower count.

### Pick a monetisable niche, study the leader

Done: local home services, Calgary. The leaders are HomeStars, Thumbtack, Jiffy. The spin is already documented in [04-positioning-and-messaging.md](04-positioning-and-messaging.md) — they charge per lead regardless of outcome, we charge 10% only on completed revenue. **That is the whole marketing message.** Lead with it in every ad, every pitch, every cold DM.

### Good content, 3-second hook, adds value

Applies, with the caveat from [12-social-media-playbook.md](12-social-media-playbook.md): before/afters, real businesses, real jobs. Stock photos are dead on arrival in a trust business.

### Trends and pop culture

Use sparingly. A Calgary homeowner deciding who to let into their house is making a trust decision — a Ghostbusters slime tie-in reads as unserious. **Local trends beat pop-culture trends here**: Chinook week, first snowfall, spring melt flooding, Stampede, moving season (July 1). Those are content goldmines with genuine local intent behind them.

### Document the process

Strong fit, and nearly free. Amr is a solo founder building a marketplace — that's a story. Building-in-public posts also do supply-side work: a business owner who's watched you build it is warmer than a cold DM. Film the walkthrough, the first booking, the first payout.

### Testimonials and success stories

**The single highest-value content type for SwingBy** and the one to prioritise above all others. A cleaner saying "I got three jobs this week and kept 90%" does more than 100 designed posts. `content-library/customer-story-template.md` already exists. Tag the business — they'll share it to their own audience, which is exactly the audience we want.

### Rule of 100 / three posts a day

Directionally right, practically wrong for a solo founder with a day job and an Aug 31 launch. **Three high-quality posts a week, sustained, beats three a day abandoned in week two** — and [12-social-media-playbook.md](12-social-media-playbook.md) already commits to that. The volume advice exists because most people post nothing; it isn't a physical law. Batch a week's content in one Sunday session and schedule it.  <!-- lint-ok -->

### CTA every fifth post, always have something to invite people to

Keep. The invitation for the next 90 days is: **"post a job free"** (clients) and **"list your business free, pay only when you get paid"** (businesses).

### Hashtags and post timing

Marginal but free. Local tags (`#yyc`, `#calgary`, `#yycsmallbusiness`, `#calgaryrealestate`) matter more than volume tags. Times are already set in [12-social-media-playbook.md](12-social-media-playbook.md).

### Follow and engage the top 100 in the niche

**Reframe this — it's the supply-side channel in disguise.** Don't follow marketplace founders; follow **100 Calgary service businesses**. Comment genuinely on their work. That comment is warm-up for the DM asking them to list. The Gary Vee $1.80 strategy works here precisely because the "niche accounts" are literally our prospective supply.

### Cold contact: framework, don't automate

Correct, and worth restating hard: **do not automate supply-side outreach.** These are 200 small business owners in one city who talk to each other. One badly-automated DM blast burns the whole market. The framework:

> Hi [name] — saw your [specific recent job/post]. I'm building SwingBy, a Calgary services marketplace. Unlike HomeStars you don't pay for leads — 10% only when a job completes and you get paid. Free to list. Worth 10 minutes?

Personal, specific, one ask, and the differentiator in sentence three.

### $10/day ads, ads are for offers only

Sound, and Brunson's structural point is the important one: **ads go to offers, not to posts.** But hold the spend until the funnel converts. Spending $10/day to send strangers to a product where a stranger can't yet sign up and pay is setting money on fire. See [11b-ads-plan.md](11b-ads-plan.md) and the gate below.

### Pay influencers

Not yet, and probably never in the form the notes mean. The Calgary equivalent that *does* work: **partner with adjacent local businesses** — realtors, property managers, moving companies — who already have the audience at exactly the moment they need a service. That's [10-partnerships.md](10-partnerships.md), and it's higher ROI than any influencer fee.

### Rework content per platform

Correct. One shoot → IG Reel, TikTok, YouTube Short, FB post, LinkedIn text. The [14-automation-stack.md](14-automation-stack.md) content workflow does exactly this: one brief in, platform-specific copy out.

## Part 2 — SwingBy-specific tips the notes don't cover

These matter more here than anything above.

1. **Google Business Profile beats Instagram for local intent.** Free, and it's where "cleaner near me" actually happens. Set it up in phase 0.
2. **Solve the cold-start problem supply-first.** A client who posts a job and gets zero bids never comes back. Never drive client demand into a category with fewer than ~5 active businesses. Launch categories one at a time.
3. **Geographic density over city coverage.** Own three Calgary quadrants properly before claiming the whole city. A marketplace that's thin everywhere feels broken everywhere.
4. **Neighbourhood Facebook groups are the highest-intent free channel in Calgary.** "Can anyone recommend a cleaner?" is posted daily. Be genuinely useful there — do not spam; most groups ban self-promotion and the ban is permanent.
5. **The 10%-on-completion model is the entire pitch.** Lead with it every time. It's the one thing competitors structurally can't copy without breaking their revenue model.
6. **Sell safety to clients, not convenience.** The competitor is Kijiji and a stranger with a van. Payment held until you approve, proof-of-work photos, and a dispute process is the message.
7. **Every completed job is three content assets** — before/after, a business testimonial, a client testimonial. Ask at the moment of completion, in-app, while satisfaction is highest.
8. **Seasonality is the local content calendar.** Snow removal (Oct), spring cleaning (Apr), moving (Jul 1), Stampede prep (Jul), furnace/gutters (Sep). Plan campaigns against these, not against generic content themes.
9. **Reply within 2 hours or the lead is gone.** Already in [12](12-social-media-playbook.md); the [14](14-automation-stack.md) DM triage exists to make it achievable while holding a day job.
10. **Name the businesses.** Trust in local services is personal. "Maria's Cleaning, Bowness, 4.9★" converts; "a cleaner" doesn't.

## Part 3 — Workarounds (free path)

| Need | Paid default | Free workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Automation | Zapier / n8n Cloud $20+/mo | ✅ Self-hosted n8n, already running |
| Approval + ops console | Slack paid | ✅ Discord + Telegram, both free |
| Email sending | Mailchimp | ✅ Resend free tier (3k/mo), already keyed |
| Mailboxes | Google Workspace $7/user/mo | ✅ Cloudflare Email Routing, free unlimited aliases |
| Analytics | GA4 + cookie banner | ✅ Cloudflare Web Analytics, free, no banner, no consent burden |
| Scheduling | Buffer / Later | ✅ n8n cron + Meta Graph API |
| Graphics | Canva Pro | ✅ HTML→PNG via headless Chromium on the box (already working, no sudo) |
| Copywriting | Agency | ✅ Claude via API, ~$2/mo at our volume |
| Landing pages | Webflow | ✅ The launch site we already built |
| Reply automation | Manychat $15+/mo | ✅ n8n + Claude Haiku 4.5, ~$5/mo |
| Link in bio | Linktree Pro | ✅ A page on `swingbyy.com` we control |
| Stock photos | Shutterstock | ✅ Photos of real jobs — which outperform stock anyway |

**Total marketing infrastructure: ~$10/month.** The only things worth paying for are the Apple Developer account ($99/yr, unavoidable) and, eventually, ad spend.

Two workarounds worth calling out because they're free *and* better than the paid option: Cloudflare Web Analytics (no cookie banner means no consent friction and no PIPEDA exposure) and real job photos (stock photos actively hurt in a trust business).

## Part 4 — What not to do

- **Don't buy followers or engagement.** In a trust business, a fake-looking account is worse than a small one.
- **Don't automate supply-side outreach.** 200 business owners in one city who talk to each other.
- **Don't spam neighbourhood groups.** One ban is permanent and they're the best free channel we have.
- **Don't run ads before the funnel converts.** See the gate below.
- **Don't build the content machine before the product takes a payment.** See the gate below.
- **Don't chase follower count.** 250 businesses and a working marketplace is the goal.
- **Don't publish `amr@swingbyy.com`.** It's a login. Public contact is `hello@` / `support@` ([13](13-accounts-and-identity.md)).
- **Don't promise features that don't ship yet.** The payment model is hold-until-approval — there is NO staged 50/50 release and there never was in the code. This bullet itself claimed the opposite until 2026-08-01, which is how the wrong number reached the live site, the knowledge base and two app screens. Marketing the future product is how you generate refund requests.

## The gate

Two hours of phase-0 account setup ([13](13-accounts-and-identity.md)) is worth doing now — handles get squatted, DNS propagates slowly, Meta App Review takes days, and none of it depends on the product being finished.

Everything else on this page is **downstream of a working product**. The sequence is not negotiable:

1. A stranger can sign up, post a job, and pay → **M1 gate + the walkthrough audit**
2. 20 businesses listed, 5 real bookings completed → proof it works
3. *Then* the content machine, the ads, the funnel dashboard

Marketing a product a stranger can't buy yet produces zero customers and consumes the evenings that would have made it buyable. This page will still be here when the gate clears — and it's written so that when it does, execution is a checklist, not a research project.
